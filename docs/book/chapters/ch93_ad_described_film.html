Chapter 93

The Described Film: Two FFmpeg Graphs

Learning Objectives

  • Mix a spoken track over a soundtrack without attenuating either
  • Hold a picture with tpad and silence the film's own audio over exactly the same stretch
  • Keep the film's picture when a re-encode is not needed
  • Design a fallback chain so a legitimate input is not refused
  • Report FFmpeg's own complaint when every way fails

93.1 One Output, Two Shapes

The watchable output of a run is <video> - described.mp4, and its shape depends entirely on the placement:

StandardExtended
Picturecopied with -c:v copy trimmed, padded with tpad, concatenated
Soundtrackmixed with the description silent during each hold, then mixed
Reasonnothing about the film changes the film really is longer

Both live in describe/extended.py, because they are the same question asked twice: which FFmpeg graph turns this film plus this narration into the film a viewer should watch.

93.2 Mixing the Description Over the Film

In standard placement the narration was written on the film's own clock and is silent wherever it has nothing to say, so the described film is the film plus the narration:

def mix_graph() -> str:
    return ";".join([
        "[0:a]aresample=44100,aformat=sample_fmts=fltp:sample_rates=44100:"
        "channel_layouts=stereo[film]",
        "[1:a]aresample=44100,aformat=sample_fmts=fltp:sample_rates=44100:"
        "channel_layouts=stereo[voice]",
        "[film][voice]amix=inputs=2:duration=first:normalize=0[outa]",
    ])

Two arguments carry the meaning:

  • duration=first — the mix is as long as the film, because the film is the first input. A narration track that ends before the film does must not shorten the described film.
  • normalize=0 — FFmpeg's mixer divides the sum of its inputs by their number by default, which would make both the film and the description half as loud. Nothing here overlaps in time, so nothing needs attenuating.

The video is passed through with -c:v copy. Re-encoding a film that did not change would cost an hour of compute and a generation of quality for no benefit at all — so the frames the viewer sees are the source's own.

93.3 A Fallback Chain, Not a Single Command

Two inputs are legitimate and neither is an error:

  1. a video whose codec cannot be stored in an MP4 as it is — an old AVI, a VP9 WebM — which needs a re-encode; and
  2. a video with no soundtrack at all — a screen recording — where there is nothing to mix with, so the description becomes the whole soundtrack.

Rather than probe for both, the feature builds a short chain of invocations and keeps the first that works. Every attempt writes a file that plays; the ordering keeps the cheap, lossless one first:

copy_video = ["-c:v", "copy"]
encode_video = ["-c:v", "libx264", "-preset", "medium", "-crf", "20",
                "-pix_fmt", "yuv420p"]
# Both mixes are tried before dropping the film's own soundtrack: a video whose
# *codec* cannot go into an MP4 must be re-encoded, not have its sound taken away.
for graph, audio_map in ((mix_graph(), "[outa]"), (None, "1:a:0")):
    for video_args in (copy_video, encode_video):
        attempts.append(build(graph, video_args, audio_map))
Order is a design decision. Trying the re-encode before the narration-only fallback would mean that a film with no audio is re-encoded for nothing before being written correctly; and trying -c:v copy first is what keeps most runs lossless. The chain is four attempts long and each one is a whole, playable file.

93.4 Stopping the Film

In extended placement the film is cut into the stretches it plays and the holds between them, and FFmpeg is asked to do both jobs at once. Read the described film as a sentence: play [0, t1), hold p1, play [t1, t2), …, play [tk, end).

[0:v]trim=start=0.000:end=120.000,setpts=PTS-STARTPTS,
     tpad=start_mode=clone:start_duration=15.172,format=yuv420p[v0];
[0:a]atrim=start=0.000:end=120.000,asetpts=PTS-STARTPTS,
     adelay=15172:all=1,aresample=44100,aformat=...[a0];
[v0][a0][v1][a1]concat=n=2:v=1:a=1[filmv][filma];
[1:a]aresample=44100,aformat=...[spoke];
[filma][spoke]amix=inputs=2:duration=first:normalize=0[outa]
  • tpad=start_mode=clone freezes the first frame of a stretch backwards for the hold that comes before it; stop_mode=clone freezes the last frame onwards for a hold with no film left to play after it.
  • adelay pushes the soundtrack of that stretch back by the same amount, and apad appends silence when the hold comes after it. The film is silent over exactly the stretch it is held, which is why mixing the two is an addition rather than a compromise.
  • A stretch with no film in it is dropped and its hold joins the next one, so two descriptions at the same moment hold the picture once for both, and a stop at the very end of the film holds the final frame instead of producing an empty part.

An extended render needs the film's real length, and says so when it cannot be read: render_parts() raises ExtendedRenderError("The length of this video could not be read, so it cannot be stopped for a description.") rather than guessing.

93.5 Failure Is a Warning, Not a Lost Run

Rendering is the last step of a run and the only one that needs FFmpeg. By the time it happens the script, the subtitle track and the spoken narration are already on disk, so a failure must not throw them away:

try:
    out_path = extended.mix(find_ffmpeg(), source,
                            os.path.join(self.project_dir, info["file"]),
                            extended.described_path(source, self.project_dir))
except extended.ExtendedRenderError as exc:
    log.warning("Could not write the described film for %s: %s", source, exc)
    self.warnings.append(f"Could not write the described video for {name}: {exc}")
    return

The warning is shown in the completion dialog, the film is simply absent from the record, and the two written outputs stay. When FFmpeg is missing altogether the dialog offers to install it before the run starts, which is the better place to solve it — see the next chapter.

93.6 Exercises

  1. Add a fifth attempt that hard-burns the description as subtitles instead of audio. Which placement does that suit?
  2. Keep the +faststart flag but write the described film as MKV when the source is MKV. What changes in mix_attempts()?
  3. Explain why normalize=0 is safe in extended placement, where the film's own audio is silent rather than absent.

Key Takeaways

  • Standard placement copies the picture and mixes the sound; extended placement holds the picture and silences the film over the same stretch.
  • duration=first keeps the film's length and normalize=0 keeps its volume.
  • Express legitimate inputs as a fallback chain of whole commands instead of probing for every case.
  • A failed render is a warning with FFmpeg's own complaint; the text and the audio survive it.
